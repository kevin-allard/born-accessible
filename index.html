For product evaluation

Accessibility Conformance Reports

An Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR) helps you understand a product’s accessibility features and known limitations, with details about the version and experience evaluated.

ACRs are available upon request through the Accessibility Team. The team can help identify the appropriate report and explain its scope.

For the classroom

Accessible Pathways

For older products with accessibility limitations, Accessible Pathways provide guidance to help teachers and districts support equitable participation. Depending on the program and learner’s needs, this may include alternative ways to access content or educator-supported activities.

Access this guidance through our Customer Support network. Available pathways and materials vary by program.

Accessible Pathways address classroom participation needs. For details about a product’s accessibility features, our Accessibility Team can provide you with the relevant Accessibility Conformance Report(s) (ACR).

Your accessibility questions.

What does Born Accessible mean for the products I use?

Born Accessible guides the development of our next-generation learning experiences. Accessibility features vary across existing products, and some legacy programs have known limitations. Our Accessibility Team can help you understand your product’s ACR, while Customer Support can connect you with available Accessible Pathways.

How does accessibility shape new content?

The approach begins with structured content and accessible design choices at the source. Priorities include meaningful text alternatives, navigable reading structures, accessible mathematical expressions, and experiences that support assistive technology. Our Accessibility Team can help you explore the capabilities available in your product and version.

What happens when I report a barrier?

Customer Support gathers the product, activity, and access issue so the appropriate team can review it. The team considers available instructional pathways and needed improvements. Program-specific content issues are considered as titles come up for revision. Customer Support can help you explore available guidance for your current program.

Carnegie Learning’s Approach to AI

At Carnegie Learning, we believe AI should be a responsible, human-centered partner in education. Our AI-enabled products, services, and content development practices are designed to empower students, support educators, and enhance instruction through ethical, transparent, and pedagogy-driven use of technology.

Our approach prioritizes student success, safeguards data privacy, respects intellectual property, and maintains the critical role of educators, content experts, and human judgment in the learning process. We use AI to strengthen teaching and learning, not to replace the expertise, care, and decision-making that high-quality education requires.

Our guiding principles shape how we use AI in our products, services, and content development processes.

Guiding principle

Elevating Educators, Not Replacing Them

Recognizing the central role of teachers in education, we use AI to elevate that role by helping reduce administrative burden, supporting instructional insight, and enabling educators to focus more time on student learning. AI may inform and support instructional decisions, but educators remain central to the learning experience.

Guiding principle

Ethical, Transparent, and Equitable AI

We design and review AI-enabled products and content with attention to fairness, accessibility, representation, and bias. When users interact directly with generative AI based agents or features, we clearly identify that experience. When AI materially contributes to customer-facing products, content, or student experiences, we maintain governance processes that allow us to explain the role AI played and the human oversight applied.

Guiding principle

Pedagogy-Driven and Coherent

We develop AI solutions that align with evidence-based instructional practices, learning science, and Carnegie Learning’s standards for academic quality. AI is used to support student thinking, problem-solving, critical thinking, and active learning. Efficiency and automation must not come at the expense of instructional coherence, rigor, or assessment validity.

Guiding principle

Purposeful Personalization

Where AI is used to support personalization, recommendations, or student-facing experiences, we apply safeguards appropriate to the use case. Personalized learning should preserve student agency, support collaboration, and remain grounded in sound pedagogy. AI-supported recommendations or experiences must be designed to enhance, not narrow, the role of teachers and students in the learning process.

Guiding principle

Human Oversight and Accountability

AI may assist in content creation, instructional support, analysis, and workflow improvement, but human expertise remains central. For high-stakes instructional, assessment, scoring, placement, or student-impact use cases, AI-supported outputs are subject to appropriate human review, validation, and approval. Carnegie Learning maintains accountability for the quality, accuracy, safety, and integrity of AI-assisted work.

Guiding principle

Data Privacy and Responsible Stewardship

When Carnegie Learning engages third-party AI providers, our contracts, vendor review process, and AI governance requirements prohibit the use of personally identifiable student data to train their AI models. When training our own models, we use aggregate data that does not contain personally identifiable information (PII). Student interaction data is handled in compliance with FERPA, COPPA, contractual obligations, and our internal privacy and security standards. Sensitive, confidential, or regulated data may only be used in approved environments.

Guiding principle

Respect for Intellectual Property and Human Authorship

When AI supports product or content development, Carnegie Learning requires meaningful human authorship, expert judgment, and accountability for final deliverables. AI may assist with drafting, refinement, accessibility, localization, quality assurance, and other workflow support, but it does not replace human creative control or instructional responsibility. Our vendors and partners must also follow Carnegie Learning’s AI governance requirements, including restrictions on unapproved AI use and prohibitions against using Carnegie Learning content, prompts, personally identifiable student data, or deliverables to train external AI models without explicit approval.

Guiding principle

Responsible AI Use by Our Teams

We use AI as a force multiplier for our teams, not as a replacement for human expertise, judgment, or accountability. Carnegie Learning employees and partners are responsible for verifying AI-assisted work, protecting sensitive information, and ensuring that deliverables meet our standards for quality, accuracy, accessibility, and integrity.

Our commitment

Our AI governance process includes review, documentation, release controls, and ongoing monitoring so that AI use in customer-facing products and content remains transparent, accountable, and auditable. As AI capabilities evolve, we will continue to refine our practices while staying grounded in our mission: helping students learn, supporting educators, and delivering high-quality instructional experiences built on trust.

For more information about how we protect customer data, visit our Security Practices, Privacy Policy, and Terms of Use.

AI in Our Products

AI and machine learning support selected Carnegie Learning experiences in different ways. The examples below explain the role the technology plays for students and educators.

Adaptive learning · Grades 6–12 mathematics

MATHia®

MATHia is an intelligent tutoring system that provides individualized, self-paced mathematics instruction. As students work step by step, MATHia estimates their knowledge at a detailed skill level and adapts the support and practice they receive.

How AI supports the experience

MATHia uses its student model to provide personalized just-in-time feedback and contextual hints, determine when additional practice is appropriate, and track progress toward mastery. Educators receive real-time and predictive information that can help them understand student progress and plan instruction.

Role of the educator

MATHia supports instruction rather than replacing it. Teachers use MATHia alongside classroom instruction and can use its reports and LiveLab information to respond to student needs.

Generative AI tutoring · Middle & high school mathematics

LiveHint AI™

LiveHint AI is a generative AI math tutor designed to help students understand problems rather than simply provide answers. It is designed around how students approach mathematical problem solving and common misconceptions.

How AI supports the experience

Students can receive real-time, conversational support and personalized strategies intended to help them reason through a problem and build conceptual understanding.

Current status

LiveHint AI is a beta experience. It is governed separately from pre-authored AI-assisted content because it interacts with students dynamically in real time.
